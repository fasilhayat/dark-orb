These are strong character concepts, but if you want a coherent portrait set, I would tighten a few art-direction rules first:

All portraits should use the same golden D&D-style ornamental frame.
Portrait orientation should be vertical (roughly 1024×1536 or 1536×2304).
Characters should be shown from mid-thigh or waist-up rather than full body so equipment and faces remain visible.
Priests and clerics should wear genuine heavy plate armor with religious ornamentation, not robe-armor hybrids.
Good-aligned lunar characters should feature the Bright Moon (a unique fantasy moon, not Earth's moon).
Evil characters should feature the Dark Moon (a dark gray shining asteroid-like moon with fractured rocky texture).
No modern fantasy-anime styling. Aim for realistic CRPG character art similar to high-end D&D and classic fantasy book covers.
The ornate silver D&D frames intact, but with a clear defined border so it is easy to follow and crop. Same size portraits.
No names in the portrait. I know who is who.


I would generate them individually or in small batches because combining all 16 into one generation usually causes style drift.
